What we can infer now

The marked resistors already give useful constraints. R24 and R26 are 120 kohm; R23 and R48 are 5.6 kohm. Their ratio is 21.43. A feedback capacitor of 220 pF gives an isolated feedback pole near 6.03 kHz; 270 pF gives 4.91 kHz; 330 pF gives 4.02 kHz. Those are useful receiver-analysis candidates, not the complete two-stage response.

The six auto-mode readings fall with frequency. Both 100-kHz readings were 240 pF. That makes a few-hundred-pF trial useful, but does not prove 220 pF, 240 pF, or any other nominal: equivalent mode and D are missing, and the op-amp and resistors remain connected.

Primary datasheets constrain ordinary bypass choices. For example, the candidate U1 accepts at least 100 nF with the specified ESR, while the SGM8542 example combines bulk and local bypass capacitors. These recommendations do not uniquely identify what PetSafe fitted.

Why RF frequency and physical size are not enough

For a simple resonant circuit, Ceq = 1 / ((2 pi f)^2 Lantenna). At the working hypothesis of 134.2 kHz, the same frequency could require:

Example coil inductance (not measured)Required equivalent capacitance
100 uH14.06 nF
500 uH2.81 nF
1000 uH1.41 nF

The five switched branches contain 15 physical capacitors. Frequency and one coil inductance constrain an equivalent total, not 15 unique values or binary weights. The roughly 5-ohm winding resistance does not establish inductance. L1 and L2 are supply filters, not the antenna coil.

134.2 kHz is a product-family FDX-B hypothesis, not a measured carrier on this PCB. Product-family context | Antenna design reference

Body size is a weak clue. C30/C31 have 0805 candidates but are crystal-load capacitors in the pF class; a larger tuning capacitor can still have a small capacitance when voltage, low loss or dielectric stability drives its size. How to finish efficiently

  1. Review the 12 supply starting values as provisional reconstruction choices; no measurement of each bypass is needed now.
  2. Use C21/C22 trial range in receiver analysis, preserving raw auto-mode readings.
  3. When scope work resumes, a carrier/resonance check and receiver response constrain several capacitor values at once.
  4. An antenna inductance measurement is useful only if the winding can be electrically separated from the board without violating the fitted-components constraint; no fitted across-ANT1/ANT2 reading is treated as coil inductance.
  5. Do not assign individual antenna weights or copy unrelated RFID reference values without matching inductance and switch topology.

Only a reference/equivalent-value decision is available for the 12 supply parts today. The original-value count remains 44; assigning a plausible value does not turn it into a measurement. A fabricated replacement also needs the unresolved device, firmware and layout work already listed in the checklist.

Scope of these proposals

  • These are design proposals and constraints, not proof of the original factory BOM. No reference schematic matching the entire board has been established.
  • Use X7R/X5R candidates for general reservoir/bypass work with effective capacitance checked under bias. C0G/NP0 is a useful low-loss starting dielectric for small timing/filter candidates; actual dielectric is unconfirmed.
  • The 6.3-V observed supply is not a rating ceiling: supply replacements can start with 16-V candidates, but motor transients and antenna resonant voltages require separate qualification. No universal 16-V antenna rating is asserted.
  • Package size alone cannot distinguish pF, nF or uF. Existing 0805 C30/C31 are crystal loads; the large 1206 tuning bank may prioritize dielectric loss and AC voltage rather than capacitance.
  • Unknown-value count stays 44. No automatic native replacement, no new scope batch and no desoldering request.

Primary references

  • ABLIC S-1200, Rev.6 p10: input/output capacitance and ESR: Candidate U1 permits input and output capacitance >=0.1 uF; output ESR <=1 ohm. This is a design constraint, not the PetSafe BOM.
  • ABLIC S-812C, Rev.7 p18: output capacitor purpose: Candidate U6 can be stable without an output capacitor; the capacitor improves transient response. It does not force C37 to a particular value.
  • SGMICRO SGM8542, pp9-10: bypassing and low-pass feedback: The example supply network uses 10 uF plus 0.1 uF (or 0.01 uF). The inverting feedback example gives gain -Rf/Rin and a pole 1/(2*pi*Rf*C). No exact PetSafe receiver reference schematic is established.
  • Microchip 8-bit PIC design recommendations: Recommends 100 nF local VDD/VSS bypassing. Warns that MCLR capacitance can affect programming; do not blindly assign C35 as a generic debounce capacitor.
  • TI SN74LVC2G14 candidate datasheet: Reference for candidate U2 input thresholds and supply limits; candidate identity remains unresolved at the measured 6.3-V supply. No assumed threshold used to finalize C8/C9.
  • Microchip AN710: Antenna Circuit Design: Resonant antenna design depends on inductance and the equivalent capacitance/loading, not frequency alone.
  • PetSafe Microchip Cat Flap family: FDX-B information: FDX-B family information supports 134.2 kHz as a working hypothesis; this is not a measured carrier or exact PPA19-16811 electronics specification.
  • Murata capacitor selection guide: Capacitance, dielectric, voltage and size are separate selection parameters. Package dimensions alone do not recover the capacitance.
  • Murata: capacitance under DC bias: High-permittivity ceramics can lose effective capacitance under DC bias; qualify effective capacitance at the operating rail when selecting replacements.
