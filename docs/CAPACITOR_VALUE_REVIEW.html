134.2 kHz, with five tuning controls

PetSafe's page selected for SKU PPA19-16811 lists FDX-B compatibility and explains the 134.2-kHz frequency. This gives a justified design target; the actual carrier on this PCB has not been measured. Different supported pet identities do not require five distinct carrier frequencies. Manufacturer reference

My leading explanation is a tunable resonant bank compensating for coil tolerance, component spread and installation detuning. Five independent binary controls permit up to 32 switch states. Near-binary effective capacitance is a useful design choice, but neither those weights nor automatic calibration is proven here. Equal or mixed coarse/fine weights could produce fewer distinct resonances. LF switched-trimming example

Low supply voltage does not bound antenna voltage

6.3 V is measured operating voltage, not a capacitor voltage rating. For ordinary rail capacitors choose 16 V on 3.3/4.5-V rails and 25 V on 6.3-V rails as starting ratings; existing C5 is known 16 V.

A simple series LC at resonance has |Vc| approximately Q times the sinusoidal drive component. A 0-to-6.3-V 50%-duty square wave has a 4.01-V-peak fundamental. Illustrative loaded Q=10 or 20 gives about 40 or 80 V peak across the equivalent resonant reactance. These are examples, not this PCB measurements; individual capacitors share voltage according to their branch.

Select 250-V C0G/NP0, 5% as a conservative design seed for all 15 tank parts. This is not an inferred original rating or a certified safe maximum; later peak/RMS voltage, AC loss, switch stress and package-fit checks may justify another choice.

Within a known dielectric and voltage family, larger size often permits greater capacitance. Across different circuit roles, a larger low-loss/high-voltage RF part can have less capacitance than a small supply bypass. Size is supporting evidence, not a value code.

A coherent RF design seed, not fifteen independent guesses

Antenna: 500 uH, now reported measured by the owner. The earlier design assumption matches this report. Test frequency, isolation from the PCB, instrument and losses were not specified. Each existing branch contains three capacitors. Ca joins ANT2 to its midpoint; Cg joins that midpoint to ground; Cs joins it to the transistor. The equations below assume the transistor is an ideal open/closed ground switch. The disputed transistor identity does not establish that AC behavior.

Coff = Ca × Cg / (Ca + Cg)
Con = Ca × (Cg + Cs) / (Ca + Cg + Cs)

Choose Ca = Cg = 1 nF in each branch. Choose Cs to add roughly 25, 50, 100, 200 and 400 pF of equivalent capacitance. Cs values themselves are not a 1:2:4:8:16 sequence because of the series/parallel network.

Receiver and timing choices

C21/C22 = 220 pF gives a 6.03-kHz isolated feedback pole with each 120-kohm resistor. C23 = 47 nF gives an approximate 498-Hz coupling corner with 6.8 kohm. C18 + C25 = 3.2 nF gives a 4.97-kHz output-filter pole if VREF is AC-stiff. These are linked starting choices for an envelope/baseband path, not a verified complete receiver transfer function.

The six fitted C21/C22 readings were 680/340/240 pF and 680/320/240 pF at 1/10/100 kHz. Mode was C/Auto or unsure; D was absent. They support only a rough pF scale. C8/C9 = 100 pF gives nominal RC products of 0.12/0.18 us, but U2 thresholds, diode paths and Q8 waveforms decide whether the timing is usable. The C19/C20 parallel allocation is superseded by the measured receiver split; the C40/C41 allocation remains a low-confidence proposal, not identifiable originals.

How to validate efficiently later

  1. Use the complete candidate table for analysis and comparison. Preserve C5 and all six DNP options exactly as established.
  2. For the next RF validation phase, qualify the reported antenna inductance/equivalent resonance and observe carrier, capacitor peak voltage and actual switch action. That qualifies the bank as a whole instead of guessing 15 independent values.
  3. Then check receiver bandwidth/settling and Q8 gate overlap with the chosen timing candidates; candidate IC identities and firmware assumptions still matter.
