U6 and Q8: stronger candidates,
explicit pin-map conflicts

This investigation supplements the v0.3 schematic. It does not certify either device or the board connectivity. The original photographs, package geometry and manufacturer documents are kept separate from the circuit guesses already drawn.

U6 / WN23

Leading named candidate: Richtek RT9818A-33PB, a voltage supervisor. Historical manufacturer marking information supports WN-. The five-pin package and apparently unused two-pin side are compatible. The suffix and all local traces are not resolved well enough for a confirmed identification.

The selected LDO application loses confidence. Its placeholder numbering is also mirrored relative to conventional SOT-23-5 numbering.

Q8 / 372A

Leading circuit-role hypothesis: complementary N/P MOSFET pair. Two resistor-fed outer pins and the apparent common connection of the opposite outer pads support a half-bridge arrangement.

DMC3071LVT is a useful Western comparison with a stock KiCad symbol and this gate/drain arrangement. Its C71 marking differs; it is not the identified fitted part or an approved drop-in replacement.

Effect on the delivered drawing: the native v0.3 schematic, its routing source, netlist and PDF remain unchanged in this investigation. In particular, H14 still contains the earlier LDO guess, and Q8 still has surrogate pin numbers. Read the conflicts below before relying on those blocks. Neither a supervisor symbol nor a MOSFET comparison has been silently substituted.

U6: what the photograph establishes

There are two leads on the original left and three on the right. C36 and C37 sit beside the three-lead side. R37 reads 220 (22 ohms), and an empty three-pad U6A option is nearby. The middle right lead appears to run toward C36; the upper right lead runs toward the C37 area. Both left leads have no clearly established outgoing route in this image. Solder, package edges and copper pours prevent treating this as a continuity map.

The capacitors alone do not prove an LDO: supervisors also use supply bypassing and sometimes output filtering. U6A could accommodate a three-pin variant, but its connection to U6 is unproved. No capacitor value, output voltage or downstream destination is recovered by this observation.

Consequences: under RT9818, H14 incorrectly assigns ground to the candidate supply pad and treats an NC pad as an output. Merely replacing the symbol would therefore preserve a wrong circuit. The source of power to J3 cannot be obtained from an RT9818 output. Its reset pull-up and destination must be reconstructed independently; neither is supplied by the marking table.

No exact RT9818 symbol was found through the installed KiCad MCP library search. Per the user's library preference, no custom device has been drawn. A Western supervisor equivalent cannot be selected responsibly until the threshold, output type and actual wiring are settled.

Q8: circuit-role evidence and comparison

R8 and R9 each read 221 (220 ohms). Their tracks approach the two outer pads on Q8's original top edge, T1 and T3. The opposite outer pads B1 and B3 appear to meet a common copper region around an isolated, two-via B2 island. These are useful photo interpretations, not new continuity-verified nets.

A complementary half-bridge can account for two gate inputs, two supply/source pads and a joined drain output. A dual BJT pair remains possible because 220-ohm resistors can also drive bases. The exact 372A marking did not yield a manufacturer-backed identification. Splitting it into a part code and a date/lot suffix would be an assumption.

Replacement boundary: a stock symbol is not evidence of equivalence. MOSFET gate charge, on-resistance, voltage margin, body-diode behavior and possible simultaneous conduction matter in this circuit. For example, a 4.5 V drive across 220 ohms has an ideal initial current ceiling of about 20 mA before the logic output's own impedance. The drive waveform and resonant loading remain unknown. No replacement is approved and no arbitrary rail connections are added.
