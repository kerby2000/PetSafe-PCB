Receiver capacitor values: C21 and C22

v0.9.43 | 6 requested readings

Battery and programmer disconnected. Confirm discharged before LCR testing. All parts remain fitted; no scope required.

B86-B88: C21 at 1, 10 and 100 kHz. B89-B91: C22 at the same frequencies. Use 0.1 V RMS; report Cp with units and D for each point.

  1. Disconnect the battery and programmer; leave all components fitted. Before using the LCR tester, use DC volts to confirm approximately 0 V across C21 and C22. Wait for any residual voltage to decay; do not short the capacitors with a tool.
  2. Use the FNIRSI LCR-ST2 with clean tips. Check its open/short compensation according to its prompts, off the PCB. No resistor-standard or voltage calibration is requested.
  3. Choose capacitance, 0.1 V test level, and Sweep. Record only the 1 kHz, 10 kHz and 100 kHz points. Select Cp (parallel capacitance) and D (dissipation factor). Manual frequency selection is also fine.
  4. Grip the two metal ends of C21, then C22, at the marked points. Tip order does not matter. Include the capacitance units and D at each frequency; say unstable or OL if applicable. Do not copy Cs into the Cp field.
  5. If only Cs is available, explicitly report Cs and D instead; they can be converted to Cp. If D is not available, report that rather than inventing a number. Stop after these two components so the first results can guide the next batch.

These are fitted-board network readings, not automatically individual capacitor values. C21 is modeled across R24 (120 kohm); C22 across R26 (120 kohm). Cp helps separate a parallel resistive contribution. The 0.1-V level reduces semiconductor excitation but does not isolate the IC. Frequency dependence and loss will guide whether a useful estimate is supported. A stable reading alone cannot exclude other parallel capacitance, prove a nominal tolerance or certify receiver operation.
